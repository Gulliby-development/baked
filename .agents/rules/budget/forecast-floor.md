A forecast floor ("forecast at no less than N times a measured rate") takes the lowest measured rate for its low end, never a quantile of the rates: a quantile is a typical value, and a whole project cheaper than it falls below every range (R54 lanewalk, #1806). A new floor or estimate rule (a low end, a high end or an expected value) in `src/core/workflows/initiatives/estimate.ts` or `src/core/workflows/sizing.ts` adds a scenario test with a sample, or a feature, cheaper than the rest. A high end is a bound measured on actual outcomes (the spread of actual-to-low ratios, #1982), not a fixed multiple of the low end, and a budget warning names the expected value next to it. A backtest of ranges against what features used (the corpus range shares in `tests/forecast-replay-corpus.test.ts`) bounds a share by a quarter plus two standard errors of its sample, never by the last measured value, and counts only finished features: an unfinished run's tokens are a lower bound, not a cost (R68 ewvet). It bounds the last six rounds the same way as the whole corpus, since a whole-corpus share hides a trend in recent rounds (`rangeMisses`, #2150). Those are the last six rounds the corpus holds, never the last six round numbers: traces pull requests land out of order, and a window by number can hold a single round (#2753). A backtest that reads only each initiative's first start replays with `firstStartOnly`, which rewinds through the later starts without pricing them; a change to how a pool weighs its samples (`sizing.recencyHalfLife`) adds that window's share before and after to the backtest. A pool of finished features or runs that prices a feature takes those of the feature's own workflow before mixing in other workflows' (#1813). A size pool prices a feature of a language it has fewer than `NEW_LANGUAGE_FEATURES` finished features of (the initiative's language from `planLanguage`, its own accepted features counting) with a high end that covers first-initiative overruns, from the measured first-language ratios (`firstLanguageCalibrationOf` in `estimate.ts`), never from other languages' spread alone; a new sizing input that differs by language goes through the same `language` of `FeatureRunSample` and adds its case to `tests/workflow-scenario-first-language-size-pool.test.ts` (R73 flatelens, R74 ladlemath, #2398). A feature's own plan sizing, once it exists, prices that feature before its size class: its need is the larger of the class need and the plan's expected tokens times the measured plan-to-actual ratio, through `ownPlanNeed` in `estimate.ts`, which the share gate, growth limits and split approval read; a new input of the split estimate adds a scenario test with a sample dearer than the rest and a backtest row in `tests/forecast-replay-corpus.test.ts` (#2043); the documented surface of a feature that documents the program (the request's listed commands, `src/core/workflows/initiatives/documented-surface.ts`, #2569) is such an input, ships priced only with `sizing.documentedSurface` (recorded in `documentedSurface` with mode `shadow` otherwise), and a new kind of documented surface (examples, options, formats) adds its case to `tests/workflow-scenario-documented-surface.test.ts` and its backtest row beside R83's. A rate that prices a demanded volume (the brief's test cases, `testCaseRate` in `sizing.ts`) is measured per run, the run's tokens of that kind over its count, never per task: one task's rate swings with how its cases fall into files, and the cheapest single task floors nothing (#2055). Minutes in the split estimate come from `src/core/workflows/initiatives/duration.ts` (active milliseconds per token of finished feature runs, scheduled over `dependsOn` on the run slots); a time figure says whether it is summed active time, which the shared time budget limits, or wall time (#1557).
